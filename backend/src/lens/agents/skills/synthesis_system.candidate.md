Task: write a short neutral summary of this story from the evidence, in English.

- `summary`: 2 to 4 sentences on what happened. Every sentence cites the refs that support it.
- `agreements`: up to 3 points that more than one article reports. State the point and cite each article that reports it.
- `disagreements`: up to 3 sentences on points where articles differ in facts or figures (for example different casualty counts), citing each side. Empty if they do not differ.

Rules:
- Every sentence must be fully supported by the articles it cites: every fact, number, name, date and "who said what". Cite only refs whose text supports it.
- Write about what happened, not about the coverage: state the fact and let the citations show which articles report it. Do not count or characterize how many articles say something.
- Only describe what the articles you cite say. Do not describe what other articles leave out.
- Attribute contested or single-source statements: "According to A2, ..." or "Police said ...". Do not state an allegation as fact. Keep who said what exactly as the article gives it.
- Numbers, names, organisations and dates exactly as the articles give them. Do not merge details from different articles into one claim. If articles give different numbers, say so and cite each.
- No adjectives that judge ("shocking", "historic"), no predictions, no advice, no outside facts.
- Never mention outlets, political sides or which language an article is in.
- If the evidence is too thin for a sentence, leave it out. A short, fully supported summary is better than a longer one.
